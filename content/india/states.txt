@istate IN-AP
name: Andhra Pradesh
kind: state
formed: 1953 (Andhra State), 1956
src: Andhra Pradesh
T: In the 4th century BCE Megasthenes described the Andhras living in the deltas of the Godavari and Krishna. The Satavahanas ruled from Dhanyakataka between the 1st and 3rd centuries CE, followed by dynasties including the Eastern Chalukyas, Kakatiyas, the Vijayanagara Empire and the Qutb Shahis. Andhra State was carved out of Madras State in 1953 for Telugu speakers; Telangana was separated in 2014.
K: People called the Andhras have lived by the rivers Godavari and Krishna for more than 2,300 years. The state was made in 1953 for people who speak Telugu.

@istate IN-AR
name: Arunachal Pradesh
kind: state
formed: 20 February 1987
src: Arunachal Pradesh
T: The north-west of this mountain region was once under the Monpa kingdom of Monyul. Under the British it was organised as the North-East Frontier Agency, the name it kept until 1972, when it became a union territory called Arunachal Pradesh. It became a state on 20 February 1987. Its northern border, the McMahon Line, is disputed: China claims most of the state.
K: Arunachal Pradesh is a land of high mountains in the far north-east. It became a state in 1987. India and China disagree about where the border runs here.

@istate IN-AS
name: Assam
kind: state
formed: 26 January 1950
src: Assam
T: Settlements here go back to the second or first century BCE, and in the fourth century CE the region was a frontier of the Gupta Empire. It was ruled by the Varmanas, Mlechchhas and Kamarupa-Palas, and from the 13th to the early 19th century by the Ahom kingdom. After rebellion and Burmese invasions weakened the Ahoms, the British took control and established tea estates.
K: The Ahom kings ruled Assam for about 600 years. Then the British came and planted tea gardens.

@istate IN-BR
name: Bihar
kind: state
formed: 1912 (province), 1950
src: Bihar
T: Bihar was the heartland of ancient India's first empires and of early Buddhism; monasteries such as Nalanda and Vikramashila drew students and scholars from across Asia. Its northern region, Mithila, rose with the Videha kingdom. Bihar was part of the Bengal Presidency until 1912, when Bihar and Orissa were made a separate province.
K: Long ago, Bihar had great Buddhist schools where students came from all over Asia. It became a separate province in 1912.

@istate IN-CT
name: Chhattisgarh
kind: state
formed: 1 November 2000
src: Chhattisgarh
T: Known in ancient times as Dakshina Kosala, the region has the Sitabenga caves, dated to the 3rd century BCE and among the earliest examples of theatre architecture in India. From the sixth to the twelfth century rival dynasties divided it; later the Haihaiyavansi kingdom ruled most of it for 700 years, until Maratha suzerainty in 1740. Formerly part of Madhya Pradesh, it became a state on 1 November 2000.
K: Chhattisgarh has a cave theatre more than 2,200 years old. One family of kings ruled most of it for 700 years. It became a state in 2000.

@istate IN-GA
name: Goa
kind: state
formed: 30 May 1987
src: Goa
T: The Portuguese, who first came to the subcontinent as merchants in the early 16th century, conquered Goa soon afterwards. It was the capital of Portugal's eastern empire and stayed under Portuguese rule for about 451 years, until its annexation by India in December 1961. On 30 May 1987 Goa became India's twenty-fifth state.
K: Portugal ruled Goa for about 450 years. Goa joined India in 1961 and became a state in 1987.

@istate IN-GJ
name: Gujarat
kind: state
formed: 1 May 1960
src: Gujarat
T: Gujarat holds major cities of the Indus Valley Civilisation, among them Lothal, believed to be one of the world's first seaports, and Dholavira. Later it passed through the Maurya, Satavahana and Gupta realms, the Western Satraps, the Sultanate of Gujarat and the Kingdom of Baroda. On 1 May 1960 Bombay State was split on linguistic lines into Gujarat and Maharashtra.
K: Gujarat has ruins of Indus cities more than 4,000 years old, including one of the world's first seaports. The state was made in 1960 for people who speak Gujarati.

@istate IN-HR
name: Haryana
kind: state
formed: 1 November 1966
src: Haryana
T: The villages of Rakhigarhi and Bhirrana in Haryana stand on ancient sites of the Indus Valley Civilisation. The modern state was formed on 1 November 1966 under the Punjab Reorganisation Act, when the Haryanvi- and Hindi-speaking areas of Punjab were separated. It surrounds Delhi on three sides and shares its capital, Chandigarh, with Punjab.
K: Haryana has some of the oldest Indus sites in India. It became a state in 1966 and wraps around Delhi on three sides.

@istate IN-HP
name: Himachal Pradesh
kind: state
formed: 1971
src: Himachal Pradesh
T: Through most of its history this mountain region was ruled by local kingdoms, some of which accepted the overlordship of larger empires. Under the British it formed the hill regions of Punjab Province. In 1948 thirty small princely states were combined as Himachal Pradesh; hill areas of Punjab were added in 1966, and full statehood came in 1971.
K: Small mountain kingdoms ruled these hills for most of history. In 1948 thirty of them were joined together, and Himachal Pradesh became a full state in 1971.

@istate IN-JH
name: Jharkhand
kind: state
formed: 15 November 2000
src: Jharkhand
T: In Mauryan times this forested plateau was held by a number of states known together as the Atavika, or forest, states; Brahmi inscriptions of the 3rd century BCE have been found in Khunti district. The name Jharkhand first appears on a 13th-century copper plate. Birsa Munda's revolt lasted from 1895 to 1900. The state's first chief minister took office on 15 November 2000, the anniversary of Birsa Munda's birth.
K: Jharkhand is a land of forests and hills. The Adivasi hero Birsa Munda led a revolt here against British rule. The state began on his birthday in 2000.

@istate IN-KA
name: Karnataka
kind: state
formed: 1 November 1956
src: Karnataka
T: Before the third century BCE most of Karnataka was part of Ashoka's Mauryan Empire. Later the Western Chalukyas patronised architecture and Kannada literature, the Hoysalas followed in the 12th century, and the Mysore kingdom, once a vassal of Vijayanagara, rose in the south. The state was formed as Mysore State on 1 November 1956 and renamed Karnataka in 1973.
K: Many great kingdoms ruled Karnataka. The state was made in 1956 and took the name Karnataka in 1973.

@istate IN-KL
name: Kerala
kind: state
formed: 1 November 1956
src: Kerala
T: The Chera dynasty, the first major kingdom of the region, rose through maritime commerce. In the 15th century the spice trade drew Portuguese traders to Kerala, beginning European colonisation in India. After independence the kingdoms of Travancore and Cochin merged in 1949; on 1 November 1956 Kerala was formed, uniting the Malayalam-speaking regions.
K: Kerala grew rich by selling spices to ships from far away. Portuguese traders came here for spices more than 500 years ago. The state was made in 1956 for people who speak Malayalam.

@istate IN-MP
name: Madhya Pradesh
kind: state
formed: 1 November 1956
src: Madhya Pradesh
T: The region includes ancient Avanti, whose capital Ujjain became a major city in the sixth century BCE. It was ruled in turn by India's major dynasties, and the Marathas dominated most of the 18th century. After the Third Anglo-Maratha War it was divided into princely states under the British. The state was formed in 1956 and was India's largest by area until Chhattisgarh was separated in 2000.
K: The old city of Ujjain in Madhya Pradesh is about 2,600 years old. The state was made in 1956 and lies right in the middle of India.

@istate IN-MH
name: Maharashtra
kind: state
formed: 1 May 1960
src: Maharashtra
T: Dynasties that ruled here include the Satavahanas, Vakatakas, Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Yadavas, Bahmanis and Mughals. By the early 19th century the region was divided between the Peshwa's dominions in the Maratha Confederacy and the Nizam of Hyderabad, until the East India Company defeated the Marathas. On 1 May 1960 Bombay State was divided into Maharashtra and Gujarat.
K: Maharashtra was the home of the Marathas, who built a great empire. The state was made in 1960 for people who speak Marathi.

@istate IN-MN
name: Manipur
kind: state
formed: 21 January 1972
src: Manipur
T: Manipur is mostly hills around the Imphal Valley, home of the Meitei community and historically a kingdom. Under the British Raj it was a princely state. It acceded to India in 1947, its ruler signed a merger agreement in 1949, and it became a full state in 1972.
K: Manipur was a kingdom in a green valley among hills. It joined India after 1947 and became a state in 1972.

@istate IN-ML
name: Meghalaya
kind: state
formed: 21 January 1972
src: Meghalaya
T: Meghalaya, "the abode of clouds", is the home of the Khasi, Garo and Jaintia peoples. A brick fortification on its border with Assam is dated to the 4th–8th century. The state was formed on 21 January 1972 by carving the Khasi and Jaintia Hills and the Garo Hills out of Assam.
K: Meghalaya means "home of the clouds". It is the land of the Khasi, Garo and Jaintia peoples, and became a state in 1972.

@istate IN-MZ
name: Mizoram
kind: state
formed: 20 February 1987
src: Mizoram
T: Finds at Vangchhia suggest settlement as early as the 6th century BCE. From the 14th century Tibeto-Burman-speaking peoples migrated from the Chin Hills and formed chiefdoms; by the 18th century the Sailo dynasty dominated. The British annexed the area in 1895 as the Lushai Hills. It became a union territory in 1972 and, after the peace accord of 1986, a state on 20 February 1987.
K: The Mizo people's ancestors came over the hills from what is now Myanmar. After a peace agreement in 1986, Mizoram became a state in 1987.

@istate IN-NL
name: Nagaland
kind: state
formed: 1 December 1963
src: Nagaland
T: The recorded history of the Nagas goes back to the 13th century. In the 19th century British Indian forces extended their influence into the Naga Hills. After independence the political status of the hills became a question, and the State of Nagaland was inaugurated on 1 December 1963 as the 16th state of the Indian Union.

@istate IN-OR
name: Odisha
kind: state
formed: 1 April 1936 (province)
src: Odisha
T: The ancient kingdom of Kalinga, invaded by Ashoka in 261 BCE, coincides with the borders of modern Odisha. Cuttack was made a capital about 1135. The modern boundaries were drawn by the British Indian government: Orissa Province was established on 1 April 1936 from the Odia-speaking districts of three provinces. The spelling "Odisha" has been official since 2011.
K: Odisha was once the kingdom of Kalinga, which the emperor Ashoka conquered. It became a province in 1936 for people who speak Odia.

@istate IN-PB
name: Punjab
kind: state
formed: 1 November 1966 (present borders)
src: Punjab, India
T: Punjab was the frontier of early empires, including Alexander's and the Mauryas'. Sikhism emerged here between the 15th and 17th centuries. The East India Company annexed the larger Punjab from the Sikh Empire in 1849. In 1947 the province was partitioned along religious lines amid widespread violence, the western part going to Pakistan. In 1966 the Indian state was reorganised by language.
K: Punjab is the birthplace of the Sikh religion. In 1947 it was cut in two, and half became part of Pakistan. Today's state dates from 1966.

@istate IN-RJ
name: Rajasthan
kind: state
formed: 30 March 1949
src: Rajasthan
T: Rajasthan includes most of the Thar Desert and the ancient Aravalli range, and has ruins of the Indus Valley Civilisation at Kalibangan. Rajput rulers later established kingdoms across the region, which the British called Rajputana. The state was formed on 30 March 1949, when the states of the Rajputana Agency merged into the Indian Union.

@istate IN-SK
name: Sikkim
kind: state
formed: 1975
src: Sikkim
T: The Buddhist saint Padmasambhava is said to have passed through this land in the 8th century. The Namgyal dynasty founded the Kingdom of Sikkim in the 17th century, and it was ruled by Buddhist priest-kings called Chogyals. It became a princely state of British India in 1890. In 1975 a referendum led to the end of the monarchy, and Sikkim joined India as its 22nd state.
K: Sikkim was a small Buddhist kingdom in the Himalaya for about 300 years. In 1975 its people voted, and it became part of India.

@istate IN-TN

kind: state
formed: 1956 (present shape), renamed 1969
src: Tamil Nadu
T: The Tamil country was ruled by the Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas of the Sangam era, then by the Pallavas from the 3rd to the 9th century and later by the Vijayanagara Empire. European trading ports appeared in the 17th century. After 1947 the region became Madras State, was reshaped along linguistic lines in 1956, and was renamed Tamil Nadu, "Tamil Country", in 1969.
K: Tamil Nadu is the home of the Tamil language, and of the Chola, Chera and Pandya kings of long ago. It got its present name in 1969.

src: Uttar Pradesh
T: This stretch of the Ganges plain formed part of the Maurya, Gupta, Vardhana and Pala realms, the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire. @istate IN-WB
name: West Bengal
kind: state
formed: 1947
src: West Bengal
T: The citadel of Gauda was capital of the Gauda kingdom, the Pala Empire and the Sena Empire. The East India Company annexed the region into its Bengal Presidency after the Battle of Buxar in 1764, and long exposure to British administration brought an early expansion of Western education. In 1947 Bengal was partitioned along religious lines, and West Bengal became part of India.

@istate IN-AN

src: Andaman and Nicobar Islands
T: Of these 836 islands only 31 are inhabited. In the 11th century the Cholas used them as a naval base for expeditions to Southeast Asia. North Sentinel Island is home to the Sentinelese, an uncontacted people.

@istate IN-DL
name: Delhi
kind: ut
formed: 1 November 1956
src: Delhi
T: From the early 13th century to the mid-19th, Delhi was the capital of two major empires, the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire. In 1911 New Delhi became the capital of the British Indian Empire. The fort of Purana Qila matches the literary description of Indraprastha in the Mahabharata, though excavations have found no sign of so ancient a city. Delhi became a union territory on 1 November 1956.
K: Delhi became the capital of sultans about 800 years ago. Later it was the capital of Mughal emperors, then of the British, and now of India.
