# Young Earth: from the planet's formation to the eve of complex animal life.

S: The Hadean is the first of Earth's four geologic eons. It runs from the planet's formation, dated by the oldest solid material in the solar system to about 4,567 million years ago, to 4,031 million years ago, the age of the oldest intact rock formations known on Earth.
K: The Hadean is the very first chapter of Earth's story. Almost no rock from it survives, so scientists read it from tiny crystals, meteorites and the Moon.
B: The first magma ocean cooled to solid rock within about 5 million years, leaving a thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide, hydrogen and water vapour. How much continental crust the Hadean Earth had is not settled: different models give very different answers.

K: Giant crashes melted the outside of the young Earth, so the surface was an ocean of glowing melted rock. It cooled and hardened surprisingly fast.

S: Life may be as old as 4.1 billion years, on the evidence of carbon inside a single zircon grain from Australia. Rocks 3.7 billion years old in Greenland hold further chemical traces, and the oldest direct fossils are 3.48-billion-year-old stromatolites.
K: Nobody knows exactly when life began. The oldest fossils anyone has found are 3.48 billion years old, and chemical clues suggest life could be even older.
B: The prevailing view is that life did not begin in a single event. It emerged through steps of growing complexity: a habitable planet, organic molecules, molecules that could copy themselves, and cell membranes.
