# Life: the Phanerozoic before humans.

S: The Paleozoic era began 538.8 million years ago and ended 251.902 million years ago. It has six periods: Cambrian, Ordovician, Silurian, Devonian, Carboniferous and Permian.

S: The Mesozoic era lasted from about 252 to 66 million years ago, through the Triassic, Jurassic and Cretaceous periods. It had a hot greenhouse climate, the supercontinent Pangaea broke up, and dinosaurs and their relatives dominated the land.
K: The Mesozoic was the time of the dinosaurs. The world was hot, and the giant continent Pangaea slowly split into pieces.

S: The Cambrian period lasted from 538.8 to 486.85 million years ago. It opened with the Cambrian explosion, a sudden diversification of complex animal life in the fossil record. Most continents lay in the southern hemisphere, surrounded by the Panthalassa Ocean.
K: In the Cambrian, the seas suddenly filled with new kinds of animals, many with shells, legs and eyes. Scientists call it the Cambrian explosion.

S: The Ordovician ran from 486.85 to 443.1 million years ago. Marine animal life diversified enormously, then was cut down about 445 million years ago by the first of the five great mass extinctions, often ranked the second largest known.
K: Sea animals of many new kinds spread through the oceans. Then the first of five great die-offs wiped out a huge share of them.

@era silurian
name: Silurian
kid: Silurian: life creeps ashore
level: 2
group: life
span: 443.1 mya..419.62 mya
src: Silurian
S: The Silurian, from 443.1 to 419.62 million years ago, is the shortest period of the Paleozoic. Life was establishing itself on land: plants with internal plumbing emerged from simpler ones, moss-like miniature forests lined lakes and streams, and millipede, spider and insect relatives became true land animals.
K: The Silurian was short, as these things go. Tiny moss-like forests grew beside lakes and streams, and small creepy-crawlies became the first true land animals.

@era devonian

src: Devonian

S: The Devonian lasted from 419.62 to 358.86 million years ago. The first forests took shape on land late in the period. Tiktaalik, a lobe-finned fish from about 375 million years ago, had many features of four-legged animals. A mass extinction about 372 million years ago killed perhaps 40 percent of marine species.
K: In the Devonian the first real forests grew. Some fish had sturdy fins a little like legs. One of them was Tiktaalik. Then another great die-off hit the seas.

S: The Carboniferous spans 358.86 to 298.9 million years ago. Its name means coal-bearing, after the many coal beds formed around the world at that time. A long ice age that began late in the Devonian kept large ice sheets on the land.
K: The name Carboniferous means "coal-bearing". Much of the world's coal formed then. It was also an ice age, with great ice sheets on the land.

S: The Permian–Triassic extinction, at the boundary of the two periods, is the most severe extinction known. It ended 57 percent of biological families, 81 percent of marine species and 70 percent of land vertebrate species.
K: About 252 million years ago, most kinds of living things died out, in the sea and on land. It is the worst die-off Earth has ever had. Life took a very long time to recover.

S: The Cretaceous, from about 143.1 to 66 million years ago, is the longest geological period of the Phanerozoic eon, at around 77.1 million years. Its name comes from the Latin for chalk. Flowering plants appeared early in the period and were the dominant plants on Earth by its end.
K: The Cretaceous was the last and longest time of the dinosaurs. Flowers appeared and spread. Thick layers of chalk built up on the sea floor.
