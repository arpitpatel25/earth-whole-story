S: The Lower Paleolithic is the earliest part of the Old Stone Age. It runs from about 3.3 million years ago, when the first evidence of stone tools appears, to about 300,000 years ago, and covers the Oldowan and Acheulean toolmaking traditions.
K: The Old Stone Age began when early humans first shaped stones into tools, about 3.3 million years ago. This first part of it lasted three million years.

K: In the middle of the Old Stone Age, our own kind of human appeared in Africa. Other kinds, such as the Neanderthals, were living at the same time.

S: The Upper Paleolithic is the last part of the Old Stone Age, very broadly from 50,000 to 12,000 years ago. Around its start there was a marked increase in the variety of things people made, which some theories link to the appearance of fully modern behaviour.
K: About 50,000 years ago, people began making many more kinds of things than ever before. This last part of the Old Stone Age ended when the ice age did.

S: As the ice melted the sea rose and drowned wide plains. Doggerland, between Britain and the continent, was inundated between 10,000 and 7,000 years ago. S: During the African humid period much of the Sahara was covered by grasses, trees and lakes. It began about 14,600 to 14,500 years ago and ended 6,000 to 5,000 years ago, driven by changes in Earth's tilt that strengthened the African monsoon.
