@era big-bang
name: The Big Bang
kid: The Big Bang
level: 2
group: universe
age: 0..1
focus-age: 0
src: Big Bang
src: Age of the universe
S: The Big Bang theory describes how the universe expanded from an early state of high density and temperature. Measurements from the Planck space telescope put its age at 13.8 billion years.
K: The Big Bang is the name for how the universe began: everything packed hot and close, then spreading out. That was 13.8 billion years ago.
B: The theory explains several things astronomers can measure today: how much hydrogen and helium there is, the faint microwave glow that fills the sky, and the way distant galaxies are moving apart.
B: It does not describe an explosion into empty space. Space itself has been stretching, and it still is.

@era first-atoms
name: First atoms and the cosmic microwave background
short: First atoms
kid: The first atoms
level: 2
group: universe
age: 370000..3000000
src: Recombination (cosmology)
src: Cosmic microwave background
src: Chronology of the universe
S: About 378,000 years after the Big Bang, electrons and protons first joined into neutral hydrogen atoms. Light could then travel freely, and that light still fills the sky as the cosmic microwave background.
K: When the universe had cooled enough, the first atoms formed and the fog cleared. The light set free then is still travelling. Radio telescopes can pick up its faint glow all over the sky.
B: Within about 3 million years that glow had been stretched by the expansion of space out of visible light and into infrared. Today it is strongest as microwaves.

@era cosmic-dark-ages
name: Cosmic dark ages
kid: The cosmic dark ages
level: 2
group: universe
age: 3000000..300000000
src: Chronology of the universe
S: Once the afterglow of the first atoms had faded from visible light, nothing shone. The universe cooled from about 4,000 kelvin to about 60 kelvin while gravity slowly gathered hydrogen into denser clouds.
K: After the first glow faded, the universe went dark. There was gas, but no stars to light it. Very slowly, gravity pulled the gas into clumps.

name: First stars

level: 2
group: universe
age: 300000000..400000000
src: Chronology of the universe
src: Reionization
S: The first generation of stars formed within a few hundred million years of the beginning; one analysis of Planck telescope data suggests from around 300 million years. Their light began to strip electrons from hydrogen again, a change called reionization.
K: Gas clouds squeezed tight enough to catch fire as stars. Nobody has seen these very first stars yet. Scientists work out when they formed from the marks their light left behind.

@era milky-way-forms
name: The Milky Way forms
short: Milky Way
kid: Our galaxy takes shape
level: 2
group: universe
span: 13.4 bya..9 bya
src: Milky Way
S: The oldest stars in the Milky Way probably formed shortly after the dark ages. They are spread in a ball around the galaxy, which suggests it began roughly spherical before flattening into the spiral disc where later stars were born.
K: Our galaxy, the Milky Way, began as a big ball of stars. Later it flattened into a spinning disc with spiral arms. Its oldest stars are almost as old as the universe.

@era heavy-elements
name: Heavy elements from stars
short: Star-made elements
kid: Stars make new ingredients
level: 2
group: universe
span: 9 bya..4.6 bya
src: Stellar nucleosynthesis
S: The Big Bang made hydrogen, helium and a little lithium. Every heavier element was made later by nuclear reactions inside stars and in the explosions of massive stars, called supernovae, which scattered the new elements through space.
K: The Big Bang only made the lightest ingredients. Stars cooked up the rest, like carbon, oxygen and iron, and exploding stars spread them around. The iron in your blood was made in a star.

span: 4.6 bya..4.54 bya
src: Formation and evolution of the Solar System
src: Age of Earth
S: About 4.6 billion years ago part of a giant cloud of gas and dust collapsed under its own gravity. Most of it became the Sun; the rest flattened into a disc in which planets, moons and asteroids grew by collision in under 100 million years.
