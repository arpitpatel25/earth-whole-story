T: Göbekli Tepe, in today's Turkey, was inhabited from around 9500 BCE to at least 8000 BCE. It is known for large circular structures holding massive stone pillars, among the world's oldest known megaliths. Many pillars carry reliefs of wild animals.
K: More than 11,000 years ago, people raised rings of giant stone pillars on a hill in what is now Turkey, and carved wild animals on them. They are some of the oldest big stone monuments in the world.

T: Stonehenge, on Salisbury Plain in England, was built in several phases beginning about 3100 BC. Its outer ring is of standing stones about four metres high, each weighing around 25 tons. The whole monument is aligned towards sunrise on the summer solstice and sunset on the winter solstice.
K: Stonehenge is a ring of huge standing stones in England, begun about 5,000 years ago. Each big stone weighs about 25 tons. The stones line up with the sunrise on the longest day of the year.

T: The Great Pyramid of Giza is the largest of the Egyptian pyramids, built about 2600 BC over a period of about 26 years as the tomb of the pharaoh Khufu. It first stood 146.6 metres high. It is the oldest of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, and the only one that remains largely intact.
K: The Great Pyramid in Egypt was built about 4,600 years ago as a tomb for a king named Khufu. It took about 26 years to build and was 146 metres tall. It is the only one of the seven wonders of the ancient world still standing.

K: When Hagia Sophia was finished in the year 537, it was the biggest indoor space in the world, under a giant dome. It was a church for centuries, later a museum, and is a mosque today.

T: Angkor Wat in Cambodia was constructed between 1113 and 1150 during the reign of the Khmer king Suryavarman II, as a Hindu temple dedicated to Vishnu. It later became a Buddhist temple. Its site covers 162.6 hectares, and it is the largest religious complex in the world.
K: Angkor Wat in Cambodia is the biggest religious building in the world. A Khmer king had it built about 900 years ago as a Hindu temple. Later it became a Buddhist temple.

T: Great Zimbabwe, in the south-eastern hills of today's Zimbabwe, was settled from around 1000 AD and was the capital of a kingdom from the 13th century. T: Machu Picchu is an Inca citadel on a mountain ridge 2,430 metres up in the Andes of Peru. It is estimated to have been built around 1450 and is believed to have been an estate of the Inca emperor Pachacuti, though no written record of the time confirms it. It was abandoned roughly a century later.
