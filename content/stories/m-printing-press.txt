T: Printing began in China, with woodblocks: a whole page carved into one block, inked, and pressed onto paper.
T: A copy of the Diamond Sutra found among the manuscripts of Dunhuang carries a date, 11 May 868. It is broadly considered the oldest printed book that survives, though earlier printed material exists.
K: Printing began in China. A printer carved a whole page into a block of wood, covered it in ink and pressed paper onto it.
K: A printed Buddhist text found at Dunhuang in China has a date on it: the year 868. It is the oldest dated printed book in the world.

@scene pp-03

title: One piece for each character
y: 1039
to: 1048
cam: 34, 112, 6500
src: Bi Sheng
T: Between 1039 and 1048 a Chinese artisan, Bi Sheng, invented the world's first movable type: small tiles of fired clay, one for each character, that could be set, printed and used again.
T: Bi Sheng was a commoner, and almost nothing is known of his life. His invention was recorded only in the Dream Pool Essays of the scholar-official Shen Kuo.
K: About a thousand years ago, a Chinese craftsman named Bi Sheng had a clever idea. Instead of carving a whole page, he made a small clay piece for each character. The pieces could be put together to make a page, then taken apart and used again.

T: Only the second volume of the metal printing survives. It is kept in the National Library of France.
K: In 1377, at a temple in Korea, a book was printed using pieces of type made of metal. It is the oldest book of its kind that still exists, and it is older than Gutenberg's famous Bible.

T: Movable type was already in use in East Asia. What Gutenberg's press brought was a much faster rate of printing.
K: Gutenberg put several ideas together. He found a quick way to make lots of little metal letters. He used a thick, oily ink. And he built a wooden press that squeezed the paper down onto the letters.

src: Ibrahim Muteferrika
conf: debated
T: The press did not spread everywhere at the same speed. According to some sources, printing in the Arabic script met strong opposition from legal scholars and manuscript scribes and stayed formally or informally prohibited in the Ottoman Empire between 1483 and 1729. Hebrew presses, meanwhile, worked in Constantinople as early as 1493.
T: Ibrahim Muteferrika, born in Hungary, was the first Muslim to run a press with movable Arabic type. He was given permission to publish non-religious books, and his first, an Arabic–Turkish dictionary, appeared in 1729.
K: Not everyone wanted the press. In the Ottoman Empire, printing in Arabic letters was not allowed for a very long time. The people who copied books by hand were against it.
K: In 1729 a man named Ibrahim Muteferrika printed the first book there in Arabic letters. It was a dictionary.
