T: At the suggestion of Morocco's ruler, Abu Inan Faris, he dictated the story of his travels to Ibn Juzayy, a scholar he had met in Granada. The book, finished in 1355, is commonly known as the Rihla, the Journey.
T: It must be read with care. Ibn Juzayy borrowed descriptions from earlier travellers without saying so, and presented some of them as Ibn Battuta's own observations. Whether Ibn Battuta visited every place he mentions is doubted.
K: Back in Morocco, the king asked Ibn Battuta to tell his whole story to a writer. The book they made is called the Rihla, which means "the Journey".
