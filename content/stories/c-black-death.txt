y: 1346
cam: 44, 40, 12000
src: Black Death
T: The cause was a bacterium, Yersinia pestis. It spreads mainly through the bites of fleas, and sometimes through contact with infected tissue. In its pneumonic form it passes from person to person on the breath.
T: Nobody at the time knew any of this. Where the pandemic began is still disputed.
K: The sickness was caused by a tiny germ. Fleas carried it, and when a flea bit a person, the person fell ill. Nobody knew that back then. People had no idea what was killing them.

@scene bd-10
story: black-death
title: Someone to blame
y: 1349-02
cam: Strasbourg | 3600
src: Black Death
T: Healers and governments could neither explain the disease nor stop it. People looked to the stars, to earthquakes, and to their neighbours. Jews, friars, foreigners, beggars, pilgrims, lepers and Roma were all accused of causing it; Jews in particular were charged with poisoning wells.
T: In Strasbourg in February 1349 about 2,000 Jews were murdered. Many Jewish families moved east to Poland, where King Casimir made them welcome.
K: Nobody knew what caused the sickness, and frightened people looked for someone to blame. They blamed strangers, beggars and above all Jewish people, who had done nothing. In the city of Strasbourg about 2,000 Jewish people were killed.

to: 1430
cam: 49, 8, 9500
src: Black Death
src: Consequences of the Black Death
T: Historians estimate the plague cut the world's population from 475 million to between 350 and 375 million. In most of Europe it took nearly 80 years for numbers to recover, and in some places more than 150.
T: With so few workers left, wages soared. Landowners began accepting money rents in place of labour to keep their tenants. Not everyone gained: for many who lived on wages alone, runaway prices swallowed the rise.
K: So many people had died that there were not enough workers. Those who were left could ask for better pay. It took Europe about 80 years to have as many people again as before.
