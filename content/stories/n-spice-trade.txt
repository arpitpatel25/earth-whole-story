@scene sp-02

title: The islands where it grew
y: 1500 BCE
cam: Banda Islands | 4200
src: Clove
src: Nutmeg
T: Until the colonial era, cloves grew only on a few islands of the Moluccas, among them Ternate and Tidore. The true nutmeg tree is native to the Banda Islands, eleven small volcanic islands to the south.
T: People there have used nutmeg for a very long time: residues on 3,500-year-old potsherds from Pulau Ai, one of the Banda Islands, are the earliest evidence.
K: Cloves are the flower buds of a tree. For most of history that tree grew on only a few small islands, in what is now Indonesia. Nutmeg is the seed of another tree, and it came from the tiny Banda Islands nearby.

@scene sp-05

title: Pepper country
y: 150
cam: Kodungallur | 3800
src: Black pepper
src: Muziris
T: Black pepper is indigenous to the forests of the Malabar Coast in south-western India. The region exported this "black gold" to the Middle East and the Roman Empire.
T: Its great port was Muziris, near the mouth of the Periyar River, praised in early Tamil poems and in Greek and Roman texts. A 2nd-century papyrus records a cargo loan between merchants of Alexandria and Muziris, listing goods loaded onto a ship there.
K: Pepper grows on a vine that comes from the south-west coast of India. People called it "black gold". Ships carried it from a busy port called Muziris all the way to the Roman Empire.

T: On 20 March 1602 the Dutch state merged its rival trading companies into one, the United East India Company or VOC, with a 21-year monopoly on trade in Asia. It was one of the first joint-stock companies: any citizen could buy shares, and the trade in them grew into the Amsterdam Stock Exchange.
T: In 1619 the VOC set up its capital at Jayakarta and renamed it Batavia: today's Jakarta. It made huge profits from its spice monopoly through most of the 17th century.

@scene sp-12
story: spice-trade
title: Banda, 1621
y: 1609
to: 1621
cam: Banda Islands | 2600
src: Dutch conquest of the Banda Islands
content-note: This scene describes a massacre.
T: The Bandanese would not agree to sell their nutmeg only to the Dutch. Between 1609 and 1621 the VOC conquered the islands by force. Under Jan Pieterszoon Coen the campaign ended in a massacre in which 2,800 Bandanese were killed and 1,700 enslaved. The remaining 1,000 were expelled to Batavia.
T: Killing, deportation, starvation and disease left the islands severely depopulated. The conquest is also referred to as the Banda genocide.
K: The people of the Banda Islands wanted to sell their nutmeg to anyone they chose. The Dutch company wanted it all. It attacked the islands. Thousands of islanders were killed or made slaves, and the rest were sent away.

T: The Dutch company sought a monopoly in cloves as it had in nutmeg. In 1770, on the instructions of Pierre Poivre, the French administrator of Mauritius, clove seedlings were stolen from Ternate. Poivre introduced the cultivation of many Southeast Asian spices to Mauritius.
