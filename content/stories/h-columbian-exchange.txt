@scene cx-03

title: The germs went first
y: 1500
to: 1600
cam: 15, -85, 12000
toll: 0
src: Columbian exchange
src: Population history of the Indigenous peoples of the Americas
T: The most terrible part of the exchange was invisible. Diseases from the Old World, to which the peoples of the Americas had never been exposed, reduced the Indigenous population by 80 to 95 percent from the 15th century onwards. In the Caribbean they were brought close to extinction.
T: Nobody knows how many people lived in the Americas before. Estimates range from 8 million to 100 million; by the end of the 20th century many scholars leaned towards about 50 million.
K: The saddest part of the story is about germs. The newcomers carried sicknesses that people in the Americas had never met before. Most of the people who lived there died of these diseases.
note: Content note: mass death from disease.

@scene cx-04
story: columbian-exchange
title: Smallpox
y: 1520
cam: 19, -99, 6500
src: Smallpox
src: Columbian exchange
T: Smallpox, caused by the variola virus, was among the diseases that crossed. It began with fever and went on to cover the body in sores.
T: It is also the only human disease ever to have been wiped out. The last natural case was diagnosed in October 1977, and the World Health Organization certified its eradication in 1980.
K: One of the worst of these sicknesses was smallpox. Today nobody catches smallpox any more. Doctors around the world worked together until, in 1980, it was gone for good.
note: The clock's year is nominal; the cited sources do not date smallpox's arrival in the Americas.

T: The potato was domesticated in southern Peru and north-western Bolivia 7,000 to 10,000 years ago. Carried across the ocean, it became a staple food in many parts of the world.
T: A people could come to depend on it too much. When the potato crop failed in Ireland between 1845 and 1852, between 775,000 and 1.5 million more people died than would have otherwise, and a million or more emigrated.
K: The potato comes from the Andes mountains in South America. It became the main food for many people in Europe. In Ireland in the 1840s the potato plants died of a disease, and there was a terrible famine.
