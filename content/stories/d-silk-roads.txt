src: Silk Road
T: The Silk Road was not a road. It was a network of trade routes across Asia, more than 6,400 km of it on land, active from the second century BCE until the middle of the fifteenth century CE.
T: Nobody who used it called it that. The name was coined in the late 19th century, and some historians now prefer "Silk Routes".
K: The Silk Road was not one road. It was a whole web of paths across Asia, used by traders for about 1,600 years. The name only became well known about 150 years ago.

T: The routes carried religious, philosophical and scientific thought as well as goods, above all Buddhism. Xuanzang, a Chinese Buddhist monk, scholar and translator of the 7th century, is remembered for the record of his travels.
T: So did inventions. The first true papermaking process was documented in China under the Eastern Han (25 to 220 CE). Paper and gunpowder, moving along these routes, changed the history of several regions of Eurasia.
K: Ideas travelled along the roads too, especially the teachings of the Buddha. So did inventions such as paper, which was first made in China.
